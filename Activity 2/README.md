** 🩷 About This Activity**

For this activity, I was asked to build a small personal visual identity using only a color palette and a set of typefaces, then apply that identity across a few connected outputs — a personal logo, a header, and a tagline design.

**Preloved You: A Soft, Personal Identity**

My main concept was to reflect a soft, romantic, and slightly nostalgic side of my personality, which I built around the theme "preloved." I wanted the whole set to feel gentle and intimate, almost like something handwritten in a journal, rather than sharp or corporate.

### Concept

I centered everything around a simple idea: a heart mark paired with the phrase "loved by me, now for you." I liked that this phrase could work both literally, tied to the "preloved" theme, and personally, as a small reflection of how I see myself expressing care through the things I create. From there, I built out the rest of the identity — the color palette, the fonts, and the individual outputs — to consistently support that same soft, personal feeling.

### Design Choices

I kept my color palette deliberately small, using only **Light Pink (#ffeaf3)** and **Muted Mauve (#995e77)**. I chose these two because together they felt soft without being too pale, and elegant without feeling cold. The lighter pink works well as a background or accent, while the deeper mauve carries most of the text and logo details, giving the set enough contrast to stay readable.

For typography, I explored three different fonts: **Sisterhood**, a flowing handwritten script that gives a personal, intimate feel; **Bodoni FLF**, a classic serif that adds a touch of elegance and balances out the softness of the script; and **The Youngest**, a clean serif-style font that I used for supporting text and details. I also tested each font across uppercase letters, lowercase letters, and numbers to see how consistent and legible they looked in different situations, since a font that looks pretty in a headline doesn't always hold up well in smaller text.

### Creative Process

I started by narrowing down colors and fonts that matched the personality I wanted to show, testing a few combinations before settling on the final palette and typefaces. Once I had that foundation, I applied it consistently across four outputs: a reference sheet showing the palette and fonts together, a personal logo built around a heart with "Preloved You" lettered inside it, a header featuring my name and contact details, and a circular tagline design built from the phrase "loved by me, now for you." Keeping the same two colors and the same three fonts across all four outputs was what tied everything together as a single identity rather than four unrelated designs.

This activity taught me that colors and typography can communicate personality on their own, without needing an explanation. It made me realize that even small, deliberate choices — like picking a soft pink over a bright one, or a handwritten font over a straightforward sans-serif — can quietly say a lot about who someone is.
