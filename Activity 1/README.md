☕️ **About This Activity**

This activity focused on applying presentation design principles to create a poster that is visually organized, attention-grabbing, and easy to understand at a glance.

**Kape 'ta! Free Coffee Poster**

For my output, I designed a promotional poster announcing a free coffee giveaway at Arrupe Hall, Booth 7, under the School of Nursing. Instead of building the poster around plain text or a generic background, I chose a close-up photo of hands cradling a cup of coffee surrounded by coffee beans, since I wanted the image itself to already communicate warmth and comfort before anyone even reads the details.

While working on this, I found the process more difficult than I expected. I don't consider myself a naturally artistic person, so deciding how to arrange the text, where to place the logo, and how large each element should be took a lot of trial and error. With limited time to work on it, I had to keep adjusting the layout until the poster felt balanced rather than cluttered.

Throughout the process, I tried to apply the presentation design principles we discussed in class. I focused on **visual hierarchy** by making "Kape 'ta!" and "FREE COFFEE!" the largest and boldest text on the poster, so the main message would be the first thing anyone notices. I used **contrast** by placing white, bold typography directly over the darker coffee surface, which kept the text legible against a fairly busy photo background. I also paid attention to **alignment and organization**, positioning the university logo at the top, the event details centered in the middle, and the supporting information near the bottom, so the layout would guide the eye naturally from one section to the next. Lastly, I applied **proximity** by grouping related details together — the event name, venue, and date sit close to one another, while the tagline and "first come, first served" reminder are separated near the bottom as a final call to action.

Looking back, there are still a few elements I would want to refine further if given more time. Even so, this activity helped me understand that effective design isn't only about choosing a nice photo or a pretty font — it's about deliberately organizing information so the viewer's attention is guided exactly where it needs to go. Despite the challenges, I was able to finish the poster and walk away with a much clearer sense of how design principles work in practice, not just in theory.
