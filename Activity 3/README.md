**🧠 About This Activity**

For this activity, I was asked to design a social media infographic that presents a real-world issue in a way that is simple, informative, and easy to understand at a glance.

**Napakaraming Nars Dito sa Amin Ngunit Bakit Tila Walang Natira?**

I chose to focus on **brain drain in the nursing profession** — a topic that felt especially personal to me as a nursing student. It's a conversation I've come across often, both in class and in everyday life: the country produces a large number of nurses every year, yet many of them eventually leave to work abroad, leaving local hospitals and communities short-staffed.

### Concept

My main concept was to turn that familiar observation into something visual and easy to share, rather than leaving it as just a statistic or a passing remark. I wanted the infographic to walk the viewer through the issue step by step: what brain drain actually means, why it happens, what effects it has on the local healthcare system, and what could realistically be done about it. I used a wilting, slightly "sick" brain illustration as the central visual, since it symbolically represents the idea of talent and workforce being drained away.

### Design Choices

I chose a **soft pink color scheme** to keep the infographic approachable and easy on the eyes, despite the seriousness of the topic. I used bold, colored text blocks to separate each section — the definition, the factors, the effects, and the proposed solutions — so that someone scrolling through social media could still catch the key points even without reading every word carefully. I also included a relevant statistic early on, noting that Filipinos make up thirteen percent of immigrant healthcare professionals abroad, to immediately ground the topic in real data rather than just personal opinion. At the bottom, I cited all of my sources to keep the information credible and verifiable.

### Creative Process

I began by researching brain drain in the nursing profession, looking for a clear definition, credible statistics, and reliable sources I could cite. After gathering enough information, I narrowed it down to the points that mattered most: the definition, the contributing factors (political instability, economic hardship, and better opportunities abroad), the effects (a shrinking workforce and a strained healthcare economy), and possible solutions (better wages, improved privileges for nurses, and stronger investment in medical resources and technology). I then organized these points into clearly labeled sections and built the visual layout around the brain illustration, making sure the information would still make sense even if someone only skimmed through it quickly.

Through this activity, I learned that an infographic isn't just about making information look attractive. It requires choosing what actually matters, simplifying complicated ideas without losing their meaning, and arranging everything so the audience can understand the message in just a few seconds of scrolling.
